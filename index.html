    How it works, and tips for shooting
    1. Alignment. Refocusing a lens changes its focal length slightly (focus breathing), so each slice is a little larger or smaller than the last. Every photo is matched to its neighbour with a coarse-to-fine Gauss–Newton fit of a shift, scale and rotation, using brightness-normalised intensities so that blur differences do not bias the fit. The fits are chained outward from the middle slice.
    2. Sharpness. For every pixel the app measures local energy of the Laplacian of Gaussian: in-focus detail has strong second derivatives, defocus is a low-pass filter that removes them.
    3. Blending. The pyramid method splits each photo into frequency bands and keeps, per band and pixel, the coefficient from the sharpest slice, which avoids hard seams. The depth map method copies each pixel from its sharpest slice.
    4. Depth map. The index of the sharpest slice at each pixel, a coarse relief map of the subject. Flat, textureless areas have no sharpness signal, so their depth is noise.

    Shoot from a tripod with manual exposure and white balance, and overlap the depth of field of neighbouring slices. Keep the photos in focus order (by file name) so neighbours are similar. Large stacks at full resolution can take a few minutes and over 1 GB of memory; use a smaller working size for a quick preview first.
